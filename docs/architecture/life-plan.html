/life: a personal operating system

A private database for one person's whole life: body, mind, people, money, work, places, time, direction, admin and legacy. It fills itself from the systems you already use, and it exists to answer one question: am I living the life I intend to?

D1

Definition of the Problem

A life generates more data than ever, and almost none of it is in a form you can use to live better.

My life is spread across twenty systems, and none of them can tell me whether I'm living the life I intend to.

Scattered. Health lives in a watch, a hospital portal and old emails. Money lives in bank statements and an accounting file. Relationships live in a phone, WhatsApp, LinkedIn and memory. Travel lives in confirmation emails. Reflections live in Notion. Photos live in Google Photos, tens of thousands of them, mostly never seen again. Each system holds a slice, and none of them talk to each other.

Unanswerable. Simple questions about your own life take an hour or can't be answered at all. When was that surgery? How many times have I been to Australia? What's my net worth trend over five years? Who haven't I spoken to in a year who used to matter? What was I thinking the month the company sold?

Patterns stay invisible. The most useful insights sit between systems: sleep against mood, travel against happiness, drinking against fitness, money stress against relationships. No single app can see across them, so the patterns that drive a life stay hidden, even when you feel them.

Direction drifts. Goals get written in January and reviewed, if ever, by memory. Forum updates and monthly reviews are filled in by hand from scratch every time, so they're hard to sustain and impossible to compare over years.

Admin lapses. A passport expires, insurance renews at the wrong price, the will that came up twice in one meeting still isn't written. Nobody else could step in and find anything.

Memory fades, legacy is lost. The stories, people and moments that make up a life are recorded somewhere, but not kept anywhere. Family history disappears with the people who hold it.

What /life does about it

Remember

Every record of what happened, in one timeline: trips, surgeries, jobs, moves, milestones, people met. Searchable in seconds.

Reflect

Metrics over time across every area, side by side, so patterns between areas become visible. A monthly review that fills itself in.

Run

The admin of a life: expiry dates, renewals, accounts, documents, the people to call. Reminded before it's urgent.

Design principles

Automatic first

Tracking dies when it's manual. Every area gets an automatic source where one exists. Manual input is only what a person alone can provide: scores, meaning, decisions.

Track what changes a decision or keeps a memory

Everything else is noise. Monthly snapshots beat every transaction. Curated photos beat the whole library.

Keep the original

Every structured row links back to the email, statement or page it came from. Claude extracts; nothing is lost in translation.

Ask before guessing

Low confidence and conflicts go to a review queue. A wrong medical date or balance is worse than a missing one.

Private by construction

One owner, one login, every row locked to that login. The most sensitive data a person has, treated that way.

Generic model, personal content

Areas, metrics and sources are data, not code. Adding "tennis matches" or a new bank is a row, not a release.

Not in scope

  • Storing bank or service passwords. Money comes in through statements and exports only.
  • Every transaction categorised like a budgeting app. Monthly balances and totals are enough to answer the questions that matter.
  • A full medical record or lab archive. Key events, conditions, medications and documents only.
  • Importing the entire photo library. Curated selections only.
  • Any public view. A future public "now" page would be a separate decision.

The core loop

1Capture

Sources fill records and metrics automatically, all month.

2Review

On the 1st, a draft review is waiting with every area's numbers and highlights.

3Reflect

You add scores, one word and meaning. Ten minutes, not an hour.

4Direct

Set 30 and 60-day goals tied to metrics. Next month's review checks them.

D5

Deployment Plan

Built into the existing davehajdu.com Next.js app under /life, backed by a new Supabase project. Shipped in three phases, each usable on its own.

Where it runs

  • App: davehajdu.com/life, inside the current Next.js 16 site on Vercel
  • Database: new Supabase project dave-life, Singapore region, separate from the site's inquiry database
  • Files: private Supabase storage bucket, short-lived signed links only
  • Schedules: Vercel cron for nightly and monthly jobs, Apps Script triggers for Gmail
  • Ship flow: feature branch → PR → merge to main → Vercel deploys automatically

Security

  • One login: only Dave's email can sign in, by emailed link
  • Every page and API route checks the session on the server, not only at the edge
  • Row-level security on every table, limited to the owner
  • Ingest endpoints accept only signed requests with a shared secret
  • /life is hidden from search engines, the sitemap and robots.txt
  • No bank or service passwords stored anywhere

Definition of Done · Phase 1

Phase 1 is finished when all of these are true on production, checked by Dave.

  1. Locked down. Logged out, every /life page redirects to login and every /life API route returns 401. A second Google account cannot sign in. The public key reads zero rows from every table. Supabase's security advisor reports no issues.
  2. Notion is in. All 579 journal entries and 65 EO updates are imported, each journal entry holds only that day's writing, and rerunning the import creates zero duplicates. An edit in Notion appears in /life by the next morning.
  3. Five years of reviews. Every EO update since 2021 appears as a monthly review with its scores and one word, and the bad values (the 76s, the 2028 date) were resolved through the inbox, not silently.
  4. Body flows. Apple Health data has arrived every day for 14 consecutive days, and weight, workouts and sleep chart on the Body page.
  5. Travel is complete. The Gmail backfill has run back to 2006. Every trip in the TripIt feed exists in /life with matching dates and flights. A new booking email appears as a trip within an hour, and a cancellation removes it.
  6. The review writes itself. On December 1, the November review draft is waiting with numbers and highlights for every Phase 1 area, and finishing it takes under 15 minutes.
  7. Questions get answered. Ask correctly answers, with sources: "When was my spinal fusion?", "How many times have I been to Australia?", "What word described my month in October 2024?" It cannot change any data.
  8. Sources are visible. Every Phase 1 source shows its last run on the Sources page, and a failed run shows as a warning within a day.
  9. Works on a phone. Home, review, travel and Ask are usable at 375px wide.
  10. Shipped properly. Type check and build pass, each slice merged through a reviewed PR, deployed through git push.
